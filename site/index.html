What if…?

Rehearse a decision for your café before you spend money: a weekday lunch deal, a new price for your flat white. What-IF turns your own numbers into three possible futures, shows every step of the maths, and ends with a small, cheap test you can run in your Kiez next week.

Berlin, 2026

Built around the rules your café works with

What-IF uses the public facts that changed this year, with their date and source, and says clearly when it is only guessing.

  • 7% · 19%

    VAT on food: 7% since 1 January 2026

    Food in restaurants and cafés is taxed at 7%, eat-in and take-away. Every drink consumed in the café stays at 19%; only take-away milk drinks with at least 75% milk are 7%. The prices you type are what customers pay; What-IF takes the VAT out before it counts what you keep.

    Source: Steueränderungsgesetz 2025, § 12 (2) No. 15 UStG; BMF letter of 22 December 2025.

  • 13,90 → 14,60

    Minimum wage: €13.90 an hour since 1 January 2026

    It rises to €14.60 on 1 January 2027. If you need extra hours at lunch, What-IF starts from the minimum wage, says so, and reminds you that your real cost per hour is higher once employer contributions are added.

    Source: Fünfte Mindestlohnanpassungsverordnung (Mindestlohnkommission, Bundesregierung).

    The method

    Six steps from “what if” to “let's try it”

    The same six steps as your report. Each one teaches one idea, so next time you can do it without us.

    1. 1

      Your question

      Say it the way you would to a friend: “What if I run a weekday lunch deal?” What-IF turns it into one decision, compared with carrying on as now.

      What if I run a weekday lunch deal at €9.90?
    2. 2

      Your numbers

      A few figures from your till and your costs, plus the guesses nobody knows yet. Each one wears a label, so you always see what came from you and what is a guess.

      • Customers a day140Your number
      • Extra at lunch8A guess
      • One deal leaves you€5.35Calculated
    3. 3

      Possible outcomes

      Three versions of the 10-day test — a slow start, your likely guess and a strong one — worked out with plain arithmetic you can open and check.

    4. 4

      Your break-even: two numbers

      How many extra customers a day pay back the test, and from how many the change pays its way after that. Move a slider and watch the result — and the explanation — change.

    5. 5

      Rehearsal with imagined customers

      6 imagined customers from your Kiez react over a few rounds. They are not data: they help you find the questions to ask real people.

      RBIS
      Imagined
    6. 6

      Your test

      Two weeks, a cost cap, one thing to count, and a rule agreed before you start: keep, tweak or stop. Printable, so it can live by the till.

      10-day testKeepTweakStop

    After the test: learn

    Tell the coach what you counted, or type it in yourself. What-IF puts reality next to your three guesses, so your next guess is better.

    How the rehearsal works

    A dress rehearsal with imagined customers

    Inspired by swarm simulations like MiroFish, made small and honest for one café: a handful of imagined people from your Kiez, a few rounds, no hidden services.

    1. RBISJT

      1 Imagined customers

      Six made-up people, each with a routine, a lunch budget, a mood about your café and a memory of it.

    2. 2 Rounds

      Each round, everyone who is around picks one thing to do: try it, tell a friend, ignore it, or nothing at all.

    3. Rafael told Sofia: “Try the lunch deal at Café Lindenblatt, it's quick if you're on a short break.”

      Added for the demo: A new café opens two doors down with an €8.50 lunch.

      3 Neighbourhood feed

      What they say spreads to the others next round. You can add an event — a BVG strike, a week of rain, a rival's offer — and see what changes.

      • Can I get the deal to take away?
      • Will my usual stay on the menu?
      • 3 of 6 tried it

      4 Summary

      Questions to ask real customers, and plain counts like “3 of 6 imagined customers tried it” — never scaled up.

    Honest by design

    Imagined customers are not data. A rehearsal can show you questions, doubts and wording problems before you spend money. It cannot tell you how many real people will come, and it never feeds the maths. The small test with real customers does that.

    Who plays them: in the demo, simple fixed rules (no AI). With your AI coach, the AI plays each one from a short description. Either way they stay imagined.

    Add What-IF to your AI

    Use it in Claude or ChatGPT: about 2 minutes

    What-IF is a connector: a small add-on your AI app calls for the maths, the rehearsal and the report (the technical name is “MCP server”). You add it once with your personal link, then just ask “What if…?”.

    In Claude, four steps

    1. Open Settings → Connectors.
    2. Choose Add custom connector.
    3. Name it What-IF and paste your personal link from the Connect page.
    4. In a new chat, switch What-IF on and ask “What if…?”.

    Three ways to start

    • What if I run a weekday lunch deal at €9.90?

      What-IF asks for a few numbers, one at a time, then shows three outcomes and your break-even.

      See this demo

    • What if I put my flat white up by 20 cents when the minimum wage rises to €14.60 in January 2027? My staff work 60 minimum-wage hours a week.

      You see what the wage rise costs you a week, whether 20 cents a cup covers it, how many sales you could lose before you are worse off, and how to test it for two weeks. The wage rise is a dated fact; whether to change your price is your call.

      See this demo

    • What if I try a lunch deal? Rehearse it with imagined customers from Neukölln first.

      Imagined customers react over four rounds; you get the questions to ask real ones this week.
